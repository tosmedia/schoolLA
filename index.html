Four things this house is for

What a nine-year-old actually does here.

01 · Every day

Homework, before you get home

An hour at the kitchen table with one adult and seven other children doing the same thing. Nobody saves it for nine at night. If your child finishes early they read; if they're stuck, Anna sits down next to them until they aren't.

02 · Every day

Outside first, always

Forty minutes in the yard before a single backpack is opened. Eight children is enough for a real game and few enough that nobody spends the afternoon waiting for a turn.

03 · Weekly

Out of the house

Glendale Central Library on Wednesdays. The farmers market, the fire station, Deukmejian Wilderness Park when the light lasts. Eight children fit in places a group of thirty never will.

04 · When school is out

Full days on breaks

Winter break, spring break, summer, and every in-service day on the district calendar. Same house, same children, 7:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. — so you don't spend August rebuilding your childcare from scratch.

Anna Poghosyan

Anna has looked after other people's children for more than fifteen years — first as an assistant teacher at a preschool on Broadway, then in her own home. She holds a California Child Development Associate Teacher Permit, is certified in pediatric CPR and first aid, and renews her health and safety training every two years.

She raised two children on this street. She opened the house to eight because thirty was never the number that worked.

The afternoon runs in English. Anna also speaks Armenian and Russian — which for a lot of families here means a grandparent can call and be understood.

There is no second staff member and no substitute your child has never met. If Anna is unwell the house is closed that day, and you will know the night before.

Before you visit

What this isn't.

A small house trades a lot away. Better you read it here than find out in October.

  • Child care, not a school. Your child attends their own school. We collect them at the last bell and keep them until you arrive, and we open full days when school is closed.
  • Eight is the ceiling, set by the state. Capacity 8, ages 5 to 13. There is no waiting list that quietly grows.
  • Pickup, not drop-off. We collect from school on foot — we do not take children to school in the morning.
  • No care for sick children. Fever, vomiting or anything contagious in the last 24 hours — please keep them home.

Questions parents actually ask

Answers, in plain language.

Is this a school?

No. This is a licensed family child care home. Your child goes to their own school; we take them from the last bell until you collect them, and we open full days when school is closed. Enrolling here changes nothing about where your child is enrolled for school.

Do you pick up from school?

We collect your child ourselves, on foot, from any school within walking distance of the house — no van, no driving, no freeway. Call or text us with your child's school and we'll say straight away whether the walk works.

What are the hours?

Afternoons 2:30 to 6:00 p.m., from the last bell until you collect them. On days when school is closed we are open all day, 7:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m.

We collect children from school; we do not take them there in the morning.

Six o'clock is a real deadline, not a suggestion — it is when Anna's own day ends.

What happens on school holidays and over the summer?

The house is open full days through winter break, spring break, summer, and every in-service day on the district calendar. Same house, same children. Those days can be booked by the day or by the week.

Do you actually help with homework, or just supervise it?

Help. A full hour is set aside at 4:00, and with eight children one adult can sit down next to each of them. Most children leave with it finished. If your child is stuck on the same thing week after week, you will hear it from us before you hear it from school.

What about food?

An afternoon snack is provided and eaten at the table, all together. Lunch on full days is packed from home. Tell us about allergies before the first day, in writing.

What if my child is sick? What if Anna is?

Children with a fever, vomiting or anything contagious in the last 24 hours stay home — in a house of eight, one infection is all of them.

If Anna is unwell the house closes for that day. There is no second staff member. You will know the night before wherever possible, and those days are not billed.

How do I check the licence?

Licence #198022164, issued by the California Department of Social Services, Community Care Licensing Division. Anyone can look up a facility number, its inspection history and any complaints on the department's public site. We would rather you did.

How much notice if we leave?

Two weeks, in writing, and the deposit comes back. With eight places, one child leaving without notice is an eighth of the year — so this matters more here than it would at a large centre.
