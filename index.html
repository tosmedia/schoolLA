Four things this house is for

What a nine-year-old actually does here.

01 · Every day

Homework, before you get home

An hour at the kitchen table with one adult and five other children doing the same thing. Nobody saves it for nine at night. If your child finishes early they read; if they're stuck, Anna sits down next to them until they aren't.

02 · Every day

Outside first, always

Forty minutes in the yard before a single backpack is opened. Six children is enough for a real game and few enough that nobody spends the afternoon waiting for a turn.

03 · Weekly

Out of the house

Glendale Central Library on Wednesdays. The farmers market, the fire station, Deukmejian Wilderness Park when the light lasts. Six children fit in places a group of thirty never will.

04 · When school is out

Full days on breaks

Winter break, spring break, summer, and every in-service day on the district calendar. Same house, same six children, 7:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. — so you don't spend August rebuilding your childcare from scratch.

Before you visit

What this isn't.

A house with six children trades a lot away. Better you read it here than find out in October.

  • Child care, not a school. Your child attends their own school. We take them from the last bell to your pickup, and full days when school is closed.
  • Six is the ceiling, set by the state. Capacity 6, ages 5 to 13. There is no waiting list that quietly grows.
  • Walking pickup only. We collect on foot from schools within walking distance of the house. No van, no driving, no freeway.
  • One adult. Anna is the licensed provider. If she is sick, the house closes for the day.
  • No care for sick children. Fever, vomiting or anything contagious in the last 24 hours — please keep them home.
  • Not a special-education program. We work alongside outside therapists gladly, but we can't deliver an IEP.

How enrollment works

Four steps, about two weeks.

  1. You come at 3:30

    While the afternoon is running, not after hours. You will see six children being six children. Bring your questions and about half an hour.

  2. Your child comes for an afternoon

    One ordinary afternoon, 2:30 to 6:00, no charge. No test and no assessment — we are both finding out whether it fits.

  3. We sit down together

    Half an hour with you and Anna. We say what we saw and what we would worry about. You do the same.

  4. Paperwork and a start date

    Enrollment forms, immunization records, emergency contacts, a release authorizing school pickup, and a deposit that holds the place.

Questions parents actually ask

Answers, in plain language.

Is this a school?

No. This is a licensed family child care home. Your child goes to their own school; we take them from the last bell until you collect them, and we open full days when school is closed. Enrolling here changes nothing about where your child is enrolled for school.

Which schools do you collect from?

Only schools within walking distance of the house. We collect on foot — there is no van and no driving. Tell us your child's school when you write and we'll say straight away whether it works.

What are the hours?

Mornings 7:00 to 8:00 a.m., including the walk to school. Afternoons 2:30 to 6:00 p.m. On days when school is closed we run 7:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m.

Six o'clock is a real deadline, not a suggestion — it is when Anna's own day ends.

What happens on school holidays and over the summer?

The house is open full days through winter break, spring break, summer, and every in-service day on the district calendar. Same house, same six children. Those days can be booked by the day or by the week.

Do you actually help with homework, or just supervise it?

Help. A full hour is set aside at 4:00, and with six children one adult can sit down next to each of them. Most children leave with it finished. If your child is stuck on the same thing week after week, you will hear it from us before you hear it from school.

What about food?

An afternoon snack is provided and eaten at the table, all together. Lunch on full days is packed from home. Tell us about allergies before the first day, in writing.

What if my child is sick? What if Anna is?

Children with a fever, vomiting or anything contagious in the last 24 hours stay home — in a house of six, one infection is all of them.

If Anna is unwell the house closes for that day. There is no second staff member. You will know the night before wherever possible, and those days are not billed.

How do I check the licence?

Licence #198022164, issued by the California Department of Social Services, Community Care Licensing Division. Anyone can look up a facility number, its inspection history and any complaints on the department's public site. We would rather you did.

How much notice if we leave?

Two weeks, in writing, and the deposit comes back. With six places, one child leaving without notice is a sixth of the year — so this matters more here than it would at a large centre.
